CHAPTER I · 1837

The saddler of Paris

Before handbags, there were horses.

1837

THE OBJECT · THE HARNESS

The saddler of Paris

Thierry Hermès opens a workshop making harnesses and bridles for the carriages of Paris’s elite. The craft is leather, the client is aristocracy, and the standard is perfection.

WHY IT STILL MATTERS

the obsession with hand-stitched leather never left.

1880

CHAPTER II · 1880

24 Faubourg Saint-Honoré

The address that never changed.

1880

THE OBJECT · THE ADDRESS

24 Faubourg Saint-Honoré

Thierry’s son Charles-Émile moves the house to the address it still calls home. In the decades that follow, as horses give way to motorcars, the family turns its leather skill from the stable to the traveller.

WHY IT STILL MATTERS

the house adapted its craft instead of abandoning it.

1937

CHAPTER III · 1937

A square of silk

The scarf that let everyone in.

1937

THE OBJECT · THE SILK CARRÉ

A square of silk

The first Hermès silk scarf appears, printed with intricate illustrations. It becomes a portable piece of the house that more people can own.

WHY IT STILL MATTERS

an accessible entry point that feeds desire for the rest.

1956

CHAPTER IV · 1956

A princess and a bag

One photograph. One princess. Seventy years of desire.

1956

THE OBJECT · THE KELLY

A princess and a bag

Grace Kelly is photographed holding her Hermès bag in front of her, and the image travels the world. The bag would come to carry her name.

WHY IT STILL MATTERS

one famous moment can create desire for decades.

1984

CHAPTER V · 1984

A conversation on a plane

Sketched for a star, at thirty thousand feet.

1984

THE OBJECT · THE BIRKIN

A conversation on a plane

On a Paris–London flight, actress Jane Birkin finds herself seated beside Hermès chairman Jean-Louis Dumas. Together they sketch her ideal bag — famously, on an airline sick bag. It launches in 1984 and becomes the most famous handbag in the world, and the hardest to buy.

WHY IT STILL MATTERS

scarcity became the strategy.

2010

CHAPTER VI · 2010

The family closes ranks

A billionaire knocked. The family locked the door.

2010

THE OBJECT · THE SHARES

The family closes ranks

LVMH reveals a large stake in Hermès. The family responds by pooling its shares to keep control, and the house stays independent.

WHY IT STILL MATTERS

family control lets it play the long game.

THE THREAD

What 1837 has to do with the share price.

i.

SCARCITY IS THE STRATEGY

By keeping its most famous bags hard to get, the house protects their aura and its prices. Investors tend to reward that kind of pricing power.

ii.

CRAFT AS A MOAT

Bags traditionally made start to finish by a single artisan cannot be scaled overnight — by Hermès or by any rival.

iii.

FAMILY, NOT FASHION

With the founding family still in control, decisions are measured in generations rather than quarters.
